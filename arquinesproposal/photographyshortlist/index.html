Worth understanding

Why two photographers

Every option on this list includes a second photographer, and that was deliberate. The reason is simple: at a wedding, more than one thing is always happening at once. Two photographers can divide and conquer. One photographer has to pick.

Here is where that shows up in your day.

Getting ready in two places

You will both be getting ready at the same time, in different rooms. With two photographers, both of you get documented. With one, someone has to choose which of you to be with, and the other morning goes unphotographed.

Two angles on the ceremony

One camera shoots the walk down the aisle from the front, so you get your faces. The other shoots from the back or the side, so you get the aisle, the guests, and the person waiting for you at the end. A single photographer can only stand in one of those places, and usually stands at the front, which means you never see the room the way your guests saw it.

Reactions during the big moments

During toasts and your first dance, one photographer stays on the two of you. The other turns around and shoots your parents, your wedding party, and the faces in the room. Those reaction photos are the ones couples tell me later they did not know they wanted.

A safety net

A second professional on site means one problem does not become a hole in your gallery.

The only thing that differs across these five is how long the second camera stays. Four of them keep both photographers for the entire day. Together Theory prices the second photographer by the hour, which is why her total is built rather than fixed, and it means you can put that second camera exactly where you want it.
