The difference

The gap is $5,967, about $43 a guest.

Devil's Food is the better caterer. Better food, better service, and a menu built for you instead of pulled from a list of best sellers. Only half the gap is food. The rest is staff and gratuity, and Devil's Food puts fifteen people on site to Cheryl's eleven.

Cheryl's is good. They are reliable, they have cooked hundreds of weddings, and no one is going to leave hungry or unhappy. They are a step below Devil's Food, and that step is what the $5,967 buys.

The bar

Identical either way. You are buying everything that goes in the glass, and Goldie's pours it. You are saving a ton of money here.

I had a great call with Tony and I feel very good about going with his bar service. He quoted extremely fairly, and both catering companies included fees (corkage) for serving your alcohol where Goldie's will not. His $1,100 covers setup and teardown, serving from 4:30 to midnight, and the use of his bar tools. Gratuity is not included and will be 20% on top, so another $220. He is planning on two bartenders, himself and one other, and I told him a third is fine if he wants the extra hands. That would raise the base cost slightly.

You are responsible for buying the spirits, beer, wine, and any other beverages you want served, plus garnishes. Tony will make a shopping list. Buy at the Costco in Camas, Washington, since they are the best deal and you can return anything unopened after the event. Let me know if you do not have a Costco card and I am happy to go with you guys.

A few things about how the bar should run. With only two bartenders you want signature cocktails only, because giving guests too many options slows the line down significantly. Signature cocktails let one bartender prep a row of drinks that the other hands over, like an efficient assembly line. I would also put wine on the tables for guests to serve themselves, and have Tony pre pour flutes for the champagne toast so the catering staff can pass them on trays. Roughly 17 bottles covers 100 pours.

Wine glasses and champagne flutes are real glass. Plastic cups stay at the bar for the cocktails, and beer and cider can go in a cup or straight from the can.
