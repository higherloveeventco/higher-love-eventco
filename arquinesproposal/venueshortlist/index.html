Venue Shortlist · Round Two

You've now seen five venues and narrowed them down to three: the Jupiter Hotel, World Forestry Center, and the Aerie at Eagle Landing. Each one can host your wedding; none of these is a compromise. What follows is what actually differentiates them.

Numbers are built around 130 guests and a Saturday in summer 2027.

Option One

The Jupiter Hotel

A whole weekend, not just a wedding day, with everything and everyone in one place from Friday through Sunday.

Drinks first, then the ceremony, then one walk across the courtyard into the tent, where you stay for the rest of the night. Dinner at six, toasts at seven, cake around seven thirty, first dance at seven forty-five, dancing from eight until midnight. Kylie's take

The Jupiter is my pick for you. The spaces are great, but it's really the whole weekend that sells it.

You check in Friday. You get ready upstairs. Nobody drives anywhere, nobody gets lost, nobody hits traffic on the way. And at midnight the people who'd normally leave at ten because of the drive home are still on the dance floor, because their bed is one floor up.

It's also the most flexible and lenient of the three when it comes to rules. You wouldn't feel hindered by extra fees or requirements, and you have the most freedom with the dance party. You can go as late as you want within reason, with no hard cutoff.

Worth understanding

Why bar costs vary so much

This is the single biggest cost difference between these venues, and it comes down to who owns the alcohol.

When you supply it yourself, as at the Jupiter and World Forestry, the liquor never enters anyone's fee calculation. Nobody is selling it to you, so there's nothing to mark up. You pay retail for the bottles and then pay people to pour them, whether that's the venue's own bartenders or your caterer's team. The Jupiter charges no corkage at all, so even if we bring in an outside bar team for cocktails, you're still buying the alcohol.

When the venue sells per drink, as at the Aerie, everything sits inside the drink price and the 23.5% service charge lands on all of it. A $12 glass of wine is really $14.82. A $6 beer is $7.41. Across an evening that adds up.

Two things to know: a service charge is not a gratuity, so it's worth asking whether tipping is expected on top. And because you can't sell alcohol you supplied yourself, a bring-your-own bar has to be hosted all night rather than cash.
