Budget Guide

What a Portland wedding really costs.

Real starting ranges from the Portland and Hood River markets, vendor by vendor, so you can plan with honest numbers from the start.

Lean

$20,000

A realistic starting budget for 50–75 guests, with mid-range vendors and minimal florals.

Average

$28–35K

What the average Portland wedding runs.

Elevated

$40–60K+

Magazine-worthy tablescapes, lush florals, a live band, and a stunning venue.

For Hood River and Gorge weddings, the floor is $24,000, and the line items below run 15 to 20% higher. These are floors, not ceilings. They reflect a full, traditional wedding: a dedicated venue, outside catering and bar, photographer, DJ/MC, hair and makeup, and a dance party reception. They don’t apply to full restaurant buyouts, elopements at Cathedral Park, or other non-traditional formats where the cost structure looks very different. For those, reach out and we’ll talk through what makes sense for your situation.

Venue

Portland metro from $4,500–$7,000 · Gorge $7,000+

Starting at $4,500–$7,000 for Portland Metro; $7,000+ for Hood River/Gorge venues. Popular or in-demand venues can run $10,000–$15,000 or more.

Catering

From $75 per guest

$75 per guest is the minimum for a licensed, insured, and properly staffed caterer. For a seated dinner with full service, budget $100–$150 per guest. For 75 guests, that’s $7,500–$11,250 for food alone before bar.

Bar service

Bartending $35–$75+/hr per bartender · alcohol $1,500–$4,000+

Because of state and local liquor licensing laws, hiring a mobile bartender looks a little different than hiring a traditional caterer. In Oregon, anyone mixing, pouring, or serving alcohol must hold a valid OLCC service permit, and many venues require an insured bartending company regardless of what state law strictly mandates. Ask your venue for their bar requirements in writing before you book anyone. Here is how bartending services and costs are usually structured.

The “dry hire” model, and why you buy the alcohol

Most mobile bartending companies operate as a “dry hire.” They provide the professional staff, bartending tools, custom menu planning, and calculations, but they can’t legally sell you the alcohol itself.

How it works: Your bartender designs your drink menu and gives you an exact, itemized shopping list (down to the number of bottles of vodka, cases of beer, and bottles of wine) based on your guest count. You buy the alcohol yourself and have it delivered or brought to the venue. In Oregon, beer and wine are sold at grocery stores and warehouse clubs, while spirits are sold through Oregon liquor stores.

The benefits: This can save you a significant amount. You pay retail prices for your alcohol instead of marked-up catering prices, and some retailers will accept returns of unopened bottles after the wedding. Ask about the return policy before you buy.

If your venue has an in-house bar, expect a bar minimum instead: a set dollar amount you pay regardless of how much your guests actually drink.

Typical pricing structures

When you review quotes, you’ll usually see bartenders price their work in one of three ways:

Hourly labor rate: A flat rate per hour, per bartender, typically $35–$75+ per hour. This covers their hands behind the bar only. You supply everything else, including ice, mixers, cups, and the bar or table itself.

Tiered guest package: A flat rate for a standard 4–5 hour reception, scaled by guest count. For example, a package for up to 100 guests might run $700–$900, while up to 150 guests might be $1,000+.

Per-person package: A flat rate per guest, usually $12–$22+ per person for a dry hire. In this model, the company provides the mobile bar, custom mixers, fresh syrups, ice, garnishes, and high-quality disposables.

Separately from bartending, budget $1,500–$4,000+ for the alcohol itself, depending on guest count and drink selection.

Photography

From $2,500

Starting at $2,500 for 6–8 hours from a licensed, insured Portland-area photographer with documented experience and full gallery delivery. Mid-range photographers run $3,500–$5,000. In-demand and editorial photographers start at $6,000–$6,500+ for 8 hours.

Most packages do not include retouching; budget an additional $100/hour if requested. The photographer you fall in love with on Instagram will almost certainly be at the higher end of this range.

Videography

$2,500–$6,000+

$2,500–$6,000+ for a professional wedding videographer with full-day coverage and edited highlight film delivery. Videography is one of the most commonly regretted skips. Photos capture the look of your wedding, but video captures the sound, the movement, and the feeling. In-demand videographers in Portland book just as far in advance as photographers.

DJ & MC

From $1,500

$1,500 minimum for ceremony, MC, and reception coverage, though some companies start at $1,250 for a 4-hour package. Additional hours are typically billed at $200–$250/hour. A full evening reception of 5–6 hours runs $1,500–$2,000 for a mid-range DJ. Experienced, in-demand DJs run $2,500–$3,500.

Party lighting is almost always a separate add-on: uplights, moving heads, lasers, and dance floor lighting packages typically run $350–$900+ depending on the setup. If you want your venue to feel like a real dance party, budget for lighting separately.

Hair & makeup

$200–$700+ per person

$200–$400 per person is the starting range for a licensed, experienced artist, but in-demand and highly sought-after hair and makeup artists in the Portland market run $500–$700+ per person. Trials (a separate appointment at full price), travel fees, and early morning fees add to the total.

A real-world example: a party of five at a recent Higher Love wedding came to $2,925, before the trial. For a couple plus a wedding party of four, budget $2,500–$5,000+ depending on the artist. If you have your heart set on a specific aesthetic or a particular artist whose work you’ve seen on Instagram, book early and budget accordingly. The best ones fill up a year or more in advance.

Florals

From $2,500

Starting at $2,500 for a simple, minimal look. A full floral installation with ceremony arch, centerpieces, and personal flowers runs $5,000–$10,000+.

Officiant

$300–$600

$300–$600 for a licensed, experienced officiant who will meet with you in advance, help craft your ceremony, and file your marriage license. Many couples choose to have a friend or family member officiate instead. Oregon makes this easy, as anyone can become ordained online through organizations like Universal Life Church at no cost.

Either way works beautifully. What matters is that whoever is standing up there knows you, means it, and has a valid Oregon officiant registration on file before the ceremony.

Transportation

$500–$2,500+

$500–$2,500+ depending on distance, vehicle type, and number of runs. If your ceremony and reception are at different locations, or if you’re hosting out-of-town guests, budget for shuttle service. Guest transportation is both a hospitality gesture and a safety measure when alcohol is being served.

Cake & desserts

$500–$1,500+

$500–$1,500+ for a custom wedding cake from a Portland bakery. Price varies by tier count, design complexity, and flavor. If you’re doing a dessert table or alternative to cake, budget similarly.

Stationery

Digital near $0 · print $500–$1,500

Printed save the dates, invitations, envelopes, and day-of signage can run $300–$1,500+ depending on paper stock, printing method, and design complexity. But digital is worth considering seriously. E-save the dates and e-invitations are more sustainable than paper goods, reduce paper waste, and eliminate the carbon footprint of mailing hundreds of envelopes across the country. They also cost significantly less.

Your Higher Love planning package includes digital save the date and invitation templates designed to match your decor, plus matching day-of signage templates. I create your signage files in Canva, and you order prints directly through Canva, with no third-party print shop required. If you love the look of paper goods, budget $500–$1,500 for a print run. If you go digital, your stationery cost drops to near zero.

Attire & alterations

Alterations $300–$800

Easy to underestimate. Dress alterations alone run $300–$800. Budget separately for attire, accessories, and alterations for both partners.

Gratuity & incidentals

At least $1,200

Budget at least $1,200 for tips, unexpected costs, and day-of incidentals. Tipping vendors is standard practice and is not included in most vendor contracts.
