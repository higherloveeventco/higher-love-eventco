The staircase closure, and what changes in 2027

When people picture a Cathedral Park wedding, they picture the stone staircase directly beneath the bridge arches. It has been unavailable for permitted weddings since October 1, 2022, while ODOT repairs the concrete on the pier columns.

Full wedding permits return in 2027, for the first time in about five years. Reservations open on a single morning in January 2027.

One caveat worth holding: ODOT is working near N. Crawford St. through December 2026, with site restoration in the park continuing into 2027. The park will still be finding its way back to normal while the first permitted weddings happen in it.

If you want the staircase in 2027, start now. Five years of demand has been building, and it all releases on one morning. Couples who have been waiting will move fast.

How 2027 permits will work

Portland Parks opens wedding reservations in early January for that year's dates. For popular venues it is a literal line-up.

In 2026, in-person reservations ran on Monday January 5 at 1120 SW 5th Avenue, with phone, online and mail opening at 8 AM the next morning. Couples and planners queue before the doors open, because many Saturdays are gone by day two. Expect the same shape in 2027.

  • Staircase and deck: $683.75 for up to 100 people, plus a non-refundable application fee of $133.50 more than 45 days out. Roughly $817. The application fee rises to $200.25 inside 45 days and $266.75 inside 15.
  • A Z75 key with a $110 refundable deposit gets you a single outlet about 150 feet from the ceremony space. Plan on battery backup.
  • Portable restrooms are worth it. The permanent ones are lower in the park.
  • Liability insurance may be required.
  • The staircase and deck is not ADA accessible.
  • January to March dates can usually be booked by phone ahead of the rush.

Fees are set by Portland Parks and change. Confirm at portland.gov/parks/wedding.

About the park

Cathedral Park sits in St. Johns, North Portland, directly under the eastern end of the St. Johns Bridge. The bridge opened in 1931, designed by David B. Steinman, and at 400 feet it is still Portland's tallest and the only suspension bridge in the Willamette Valley. The gothic arched towers give the park its name.

Twenty-one riverfront acres, free and open year-round. The park faces west, so late afternoon light is the good light. The bridge also gives partial rain cover, which makes it more weather-resilient than most outdoor ceremony spots in Portland.

Common questions

Cathedral Park wedding FAQ

Do you need a permit to get married at Cathedral Park?

It depends where in the park, and how many people. The staircase and deck and the amphitheater are Portland Parks' two reservable wedding venues, and both need a wedding permit. Elsewhere in the park, a ceremony of the couple, an officiant and two witnesses needs no permit at all, and a larger ceremony can be covered by a special use permit instead. A reception with guests and food always needs its own reservation.

When do Cathedral Park wedding permits open?

Early January each year, for that year's dates. In 2026 that was in person on Monday January 5 at 1120 SW 5th Avenue, with phone, online and mail from 8 AM the next morning. Couples and planners line up, because many Saturdays are gone by day two. January to March dates can usually be booked by phone ahead of the rush.

How much does a Cathedral Park elopement cost?

Married Under the Bridge starts at $2,500 for the couple alone, $3,600 with up to eight guests, and $5,000 with up to eighteen. All-inclusive: ceremony, officiant, license filing, photography, florals, grazing spread, desserts, beverages, permit fees, setup and styling. See what's in each tier.

What's the capacity at Cathedral Park?

The staircase and deck holds up to 100 guests, the amphitheater up to 500. An unpermitted ceremony anywhere in the park is limited to five people. Under a special use permit, up to 30 can attend a ceremony elsewhere in the park.

Which is the better Cathedral Park ceremony spot?

The staircase is the iconic one and the one you can't reserve without a wedding permit. It's also not ADA accessible. A reserved spot elsewhere in the park is less famous, completely certain, on level ground, and lets everyone attend. If a guaranteed location and having your people there matter more than the staircase itself, take the reserved spot.
