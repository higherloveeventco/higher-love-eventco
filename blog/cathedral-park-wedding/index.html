The staircase closure, and what changes in 2027

When people picture a Cathedral Park wedding, they are almost always picturing the stone staircase directly beneath the bridge arches. It is the image that sells the place. It has also been unavailable for permitted weddings since October 1, 2022, while ODOT repairs the concrete on the pier columns, and that has been a disappointment for a lot of couples.

The good news is that full wedding permits return in 2027, for the first time in about five years. Reservations for those dates open on a single morning in January 2027.

One caveat is worth holding onto. ODOT will be working near N. Crawford St. through December 2026, and site restoration in the park is scheduled to continue into 2027. So the park will still be finding its way back to normal while the first permitted weddings are happening in it. That is not a reason to avoid 2027, but it is worth knowing if you are planning early in the year.

If you have your heart set on the staircase in 2027, start planning now. Five years of demand has been building up, and all of it releases on one morning in January. The couples who have been waiting are going to move quickly, so the earlier you decide, the better your chances.

The Cathedral Park Amphitheater

The amphitheater holds up to 500 people, it has been fully available through Portland Parks the entire time, and unlike the staircase it is ADA accessible. How 2027 permits will work

Portland Parks opens wedding reservations in early January each year for that year's dates, and for the popular venues it is a genuine line-up. People show up in person and wait.

In 2026, in-person reservations ran on Monday January 5 at 1120 SW 5th Avenue, and phone, online and mail requests opened at 8 AM the following morning. Couples and planners queue before the doors open, because a lot of the good Saturdays are gone by the second day. I would expect 2027 to look much the same, and I would plan for it to be busier than usual given how long these permits have been unavailable.

Here are the practical details that are easy to miss:

  • The staircase and deck costs $683.75 for up to 100 people, and there is a non-refundable application fee on top of that. It is $133.50 if you book more than 45 days ahead, which brings the real total to roughly $817. That fee climbs to $200.25 inside 45 days and $266.75 inside 15, so booking early genuinely saves you money.
  • Electricity requires a Z75 key with a $110 refundable deposit, and it is a single outlet roughly 150 feet from the ceremony space. I would plan on battery backup rather than relying on it.
  • Portable restrooms are worth the money, because the permanent ones sit lower in the park and are a walk away.
  • You may be asked for proof of liability insurance, so check before you get close to the date.
  • The staircase and deck is not ADA accessible, which matters if anyone in your group uses a wheelchair or finds stairs difficult.
  • Dates in January, February and March can usually be booked by phone ahead of the January rush, so an early-in-the-year wedding lets you skip the queue entirely.

All of these fees are set by Portland Parks and they do change, so please confirm the current numbers with them directly at portland.gov/parks/wedding.

About the park

Cathedral Park sits in St. Johns, North Portland, directly under the eastern end of the St. Johns Bridge. The bridge opened in 1931, designed by David B. Steinman, and at 400 feet it is still Portland's tallest and the only suspension bridge in the Willamette Valley. The gothic arched towers give the park its name.

It is twenty-one acres of riverfront, free to visit and open year-round. The park faces west, so late afternoon is when the light is at its best, and that is the window I would build a ceremony around. The bridge also provides partial rain cover, which makes Cathedral Park more forgiving than most outdoor ceremony spots in Portland.

Common questions

Cathedral Park wedding FAQ

Do you need a permit to get married at Cathedral Park?

It depends on where in the park you marry and how many people are with you. The staircase and deck and the amphitheater are Portland Parks' two reservable wedding venues, and both of those require a wedding permit. Everywhere else in the park is different. A simple ceremony of the couple, an officiant and two witnesses needs no permit at all, and a larger ceremony can be covered by a special use permit instead. A reception with guests and food always needs its own separate reservation, whichever spot you choose.

When do Cathedral Park wedding permits open?

Reservations open in early January each year, for that year's dates. In 2026 that meant in person on Monday January 5 at 1120 SW 5th Avenue, with phone, online and mail requests opening at 8 AM the following morning. Couples and planners do line up for it, because a lot of the popular Saturdays are gone by the second day. If your date falls between January and March, you can usually book by phone ahead of the rush and skip the queue.

You can see exactly what is in each tier here.

What's the capacity at Cathedral Park?

The staircase and deck holds up to 100 guests and the amphitheater holds up to 500. If you are marrying without a permit anywhere in the park, the ceremony is limited to five people, being the two of you, your officiant and two witnesses. Under a special use permit, up to 30 people can attend a ceremony elsewhere in the park.

Which is the better Cathedral Park ceremony spot?

That depends on what matters most to you, and both answers are reasonable. The staircase is the iconic one, and it is also the one you cannot reserve without a wedding permit, plus it is not ADA accessible. A reserved spot elsewhere in the park is less famous, but it is completely certain, it sits on level ground, and everyone you love can be there for the vows. If a guaranteed location and having your people present matter more to you than the staircase itself, I would take the reserved spot without hesitating.
